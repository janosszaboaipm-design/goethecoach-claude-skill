# L1 Turkish (Türkçe) → German interference patterns

Explanations follow the chat-language rule in SKILL.md; the L1 contrasts below apply either way.

| Code | Pattern | Why (TR contrast) | Wrong → Correct |
|---|---|---|---|
| VFINAL | Verb at the end of a main clause | TR is SOV, and the verb comes at the end | Ich morgen nach Berlin fahre. → Ich **fahre** morgen nach Berlin. |
| V2 | Subject before the verb after a fronted element | there is no V2 rule | Am Wochenende ich besuche meine Eltern. → Am Wochenende **besuche ich** … |
| ARTICLE | Articles missing | TR has no definite article (*bir* only as "one/a") | Ich habe Auto. → Ich habe **ein** Auto. · Hund ist groß. → **Der** Hund ist groß. |
| GENDER | Gender guessed | TR has no grammatical gender (*o* = he/she/it) | die Tisch → **der** Tisch |
| PRODROP | Subject pronoun missing | the person is marked on the verb (*gidiyorum*) | Gehe morgen ins Kino. → **Ich** gehe … |
| COPULA | Missing *sein* | TR attaches the copula as a suffix (*öğrenciyim*) | Ich Student. → Ich **bin** Student. |
| PLURAL | Singular after a number | *üç kitap* | drei Kind → drei **Kinder** |
| CASE-PREP | Case suffixes vs. prepositions | *-de/-e/-den* are suffixes, while German needs a preposition and case | Ich bin Schule. → Ich bin **in der** Schule. |
| POSSESS | *haben* avoided | TR expresses possession with *var/yok* | Bei mir gibt es zwei Kinder. → Ich **habe** zwei Kinder. |
| SUBORD | Clause order / *dass* clause | TR uses nominalised verbs (*geldiğini*) instead of subordinate clauses | Ich weiß sein Kommen. → Ich weiß, **dass er kommt**. |
| QUESTION | Question word order | TR marks yes/no questions with the particle *mi* and no inversion | Du kommst morgen? (casual only) → **Kommst du** morgen? |
| SPELL | Spelling transfer | Turkish letters (*ş, ç, ı*) and phonetic spelling | schön → *şön* · Straße → *Strase* · Ich → *İch* |

**Teaching tips for TR learners:**
- Contrast the Turkish verb-at-the-end order: it is correct in German **only** in subordinate clauses (after weil, dass, wenn). This makes German clauses easier to learn.
- Learn nouns with their article from day 1. Use colour coding: der = blue, die = red, das = green.
