## Goethe-Zertifikat Writing Exam: 6 Mistakes That Quietly Cost You Points

**In a hurry?** The single most expensive mistake is missing one of the required content points (*Leitpunkte*): nearly **1 in 3** test-takers in our data missed at least one. Cover every bullet the task gives you and you've already protected the largest block of marks.

**Most points lost in the Goethe-Zertifikat writing section don't come from bad German — they come from avoidable mistakes.** Across more than 300 sample exams from over 100 learners that we analysed at GoetheCoach, the biggest score-killers were skipping a required content point, forgetting the correct greeting or sign-off, and small spelling and punctuation slips. None of these require better grammar to fix — only attention. This guide walks through the six most common ones and how to stop making them.

### How the Goethe writing exam is scored

Before the mistakes, the frame. Examiners grade your text on four things: whether you **fully address the task** (all the required content points, correct format and register), **coherence** (how well your sentences connect), **structures** (grammar and sentence variety), and **vocabulary** (range and accuracy). In our analysis, candidates were consistently strongest on *addressing the task* and weakest on *coherence* and *vocabulary* — worth knowing where the ceiling really is. But the fastest gains come from the mechanical mistakes below, because they cost points you've technically already earned.

### Mistake 1: You skip a required content point (*Leitpunkt*)

Every Goethe writing task gives you two to four **Leitpunkte** — bullet points you must address. Miss one and you lose a large, fixed chunk of the "task fulfilment" score, no matter how good the rest of your German is.

This is the most common serious mistake in our data: **nearly a third of sample exams (around 1 in 3) failed to cover at least one required content point.** Usually it's not that the writer couldn't — they simply forgot, or ran out of words on the earlier points.

**Updated data (October 2026): it's usually the last point that goes missing.** The final Leitpunkt was skipped in **25%** of texts, the first one in only **8%**. And the higher the level, the more often it happens:

| Level | Texts missing at least one Leitpunkt |
| --- | --- |
| B1 | 21% |
| B2 | 48% |
| C1 | 57% |

At B2 and C1 the Forumsbeitrag has four Leitpunkte, and points 3 and 4 are where most texts break. We looked at why in [Why B2 and C1 candidates lose points on Leitpunkte](https://goethecoach.de/en/why-b2-c1-candidates-miss-leitpunkte.html).

**How to fix it:** Before you write, number the Leitpunkte 1‑2‑3 on your scratch paper. Write at least one full sentence for each, and physically tick each one off as you cover it. Budget your words so the last point gets real attention, not a rushed half-sentence.

### Mistake 2: Your greeting or sign-off is wrong or missing

A letter, email or forum post has a required opening (*Anrede*) and closing (*Grußformel*). These are free, guaranteed points — and a surprising number of candidates drop them. In our data, **about 1 in 6 got the greeting wrong or left it out, and more than 1 in 4 did the same with the closing.**

**How to fix it:** Memorise two matched pairs and never improvise:

- **Formal:** `Sehr geehrte Frau … / Sehr geehrter Herr …` → `Mit freundlichen Grüßen`
- **Informal:** `Liebe … / Lieber …` or `Hallo …` → `Liebe Grüße` / `Viele Grüße`

Pick the pair that matches the task and write it down first, before the body. That way it can never be forgotten under time pressure.

**Watch out at A1:** the riskiest format is the short message (*Kurznachricht*). There, **47%** of texts had a wrong or missing closing and **35%** a wrong greeting — far more than in formal emails (20% and 14%). A short note still needs `Liebe …` / `Hallo …` and `Viele Grüße`.

### Mistake 3: Wrong register — mixing *du* and *Sie*

The task tells you whether you're writing to a friend (*du*) or an institution (*Sie*). Switching between them, or picking the wrong one, signals to the examiner that you don't control register — and it drags down your task score. Roughly **1 in 10** texts in our data used the wrong register or mixed it.

**How to fix it:** Decide *du* or *Sie* before your first sentence and stay consistent — including possessives (*dein* vs *Ihr*) and imperatives. When in doubt for a formal task, use *Sie*.

### Mistake 4: Noun capitalisation and spelling

German capitalises **every noun**, and this trips people up constantly. Spelling and capitalisation together made up **almost 3 in 10 of all the mistakes** we counted — the single largest mechanical category. Writing *das haus* instead of *das Haus* is an easy, repeated point loss.

**How to fix it:** In your final read-through, hunt for one thing at a time. Pass one: is every noun capitalised? Pass two: obvious spelling. Isolating the check catches far more than reading for "everything at once."

### Mistake 5: Punctuation, especially commas

German comma rules are stricter than English. Punctuation was behind **around 1 in 8 mistakes** in our analysis — and about two thirds of those were commas, mostly missing before *dass*, *weil*, *wenn* or a relative clause.

**How to fix it:** Learn one rule that covers most cases — **put a comma before a subordinate clause** (the ones where the verb goes to the end): *Ich glaube, **dass** das gut ist.* / *Ich komme nicht, **weil** ich krank bin.* That single habit removes most comma errors.

### Mistake 6: Flat, repetitive writing

This is where the real skill ceiling sits. Coherence and vocabulary were the two lowest-scoring areas in our data — texts were understandable but repetitive, leaning on *und*, *aber* and *weil* and reusing the same handful of words.

**How to fix it:** Build a small toolbox and deploy it deliberately:

- **Connectors:** *außerdem, deshalb, trotzdem, zuerst … dann … schließlich, einerseits … andererseits.*
- **Upgrades:** swap *gut* for *hilfreich / praktisch / sinnvoll*; swap *machen* for a precise verb.

Two or three varied connectors and a few precise word choices visibly lift a text from "understandable" to "controlled."

### Your 2-minute pre-submission checklist

Before you hand in — or hit submit — run this. These are the points people leave on the table:

1. **Every Leitpunkt covered?** Count them against the task.
2. **Greeting present and correct** for the register?
3. **Closing present and correct?**
4. **One register only** — *du* or *Sie*, all the way through?
5. **Every noun capitalised?**
6. **Commas** before *dass / weil / wenn* / relative clauses?
7. **Word count** roughly on target — not far under?

Most of these cost you nothing but a moment's attention, and together they protect the biggest, most reliable block of marks on the paper.

**Practice helps — but not with point 1 on its own.** Of the learners who submitted three or more texts, **73% improved**, by a median of **10 percentage points**. Their Leitpunkt miss rate, however, stayed flat (29% → 28%). Content points don't fix themselves through practice; the checklist does.

### Frequently asked questions

**Q: How is the Goethe-Zertifikat writing exam scored?**

A: Examiners assess four areas: task fulfilment (covering every required content point in the right format and register), coherence (how your sentences connect), structures (grammar and sentence variety), and vocabulary (range and accuracy). Missing a required content point or the greeting/closing costs fixed points regardless of your language level.

**Q: What is a *Leitpunkt*?**

A: A *Leitpunkt* is one of the two to four bullet points the writing task asks you to address. You must respond to each one — in our analysis, nearly one in three candidates missed at least one (and about half at B2 and C1), which is the most common serious mistake.

**Q: How many words should I write?**

A: Write close to the word count the task specifies. Going well under leaves content points uncovered and loses marks; going slightly over is usually fine as long as every Leitpunkt is addressed clearly.

**Q: Do spelling mistakes really matter in the Goethe exam?**

A: Yes — spelling and capitalisation together were the largest single category of mistakes in our data. German capitalises every noun, and repeated slips add up. They rarely block understanding, but they lower your structures and vocabulary scores.

**Q: How can I practise Goethe writing before the exam?**

A: Write full sample tasks under timed conditions and get specific feedback on task coverage, register, grammar and vocabulary. GoetheCoach grades your writing in seconds against the official criteria and shows you exactly where you'd lose points — so you can fix these mistakes before exam day.
