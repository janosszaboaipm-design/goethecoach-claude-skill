## Why B2 and C1 Candidates Lose Points on Leitpunkte

At Goethe-Zertifikat B2 and C1, about half of the Schreiben texts we graded missed at least one Leitpunkt (48% at B2, 57% at C1, against 21% at B1). The miss almost always hits points 3 and 4 of the Forumsbeitrag, and it costs around 20 percentage points. Below: the data, the four patterns behind it, and a fix.

The common assumption is that higher levels lose points on grammar and vocabulary. Our data says otherwise: at B2 and C1, the biggest drop comes from *task fulfilment*, the part of the score that depends on covering every required content point. Better German doesn't fix it. Planning does.

### The data: Leitpunkte by level

We looked at 312 graded writing texts submitted to GoetheCoach by 101 learners between March and September 2026, 88 of them at B2 or C1. For each text, the grader checked every Leitpunkt separately: covered or not.

| Level | Texts | Missed at least one Leitpunkt | Leitpunkte per task (avg.) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | 72 | 32% | 2.0 |
| B1 | 135 | 21% | 2.8 |
| B2 | 58 | 48% | 3.6 |
| C1 | 30 | 57% | 3.6 |

B1 is the sweet spot: tasks have two or three points and learners cover them. From B2 on, tasks have four points, and the miss rate more than doubles. Candidates who miss usually miss more than one: 1.7 points on average at B2, 1.9 at C1.

### It's points 3 and 4 that disappear

The position of a Leitpunkt predicts whether it gets covered. At B2, points 1 and 2 were missed in 7% and 3% of texts. Points 3 and 4 were missed in **43% and 46%**.

| Leitpunkt (B2) | Missed |
| --- | --- |
| Point 1 | 7% |
| Point 2 | 3% |
| Point 3 | 43% |
| Point 4 | 46% |

The task format matters too. At B2 and C1 combined, **67% of Forumsbeitrag texts** missed a point, against 31% of formal E-Mail tasks. The Forumsbeitrag asks you to argue, and argument pulls you deeper into the first two points. If you want the full structure of that task, see our [B2 Forumsbeitrag guide](https://goethecoach.de/en/goethe-b2-writing-part-1-forum-post.html).

### It's not a word-count problem

The intuitive explanation is "they ran out of words". The data doesn't support it. At B2 and C1, texts that missed a Leitpunkt were actually *longer* on average (157 words) than texts that covered all of them (148 words).

The words were there. They went to the wrong places: three paragraphs of opinion on point 1, a strong argument for point 2, then a closing sentence. The text reads well, but it answers two questions out of four.

### What a missed Leitpunkt costs

Missing a content point is not a small deduction. In our sample:

| Level | Avg. score, all Leitpunkte covered | Avg. score, at least one missed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B1 | 74% | 54% |
| B2 | 71% | 47% |
| C1 | 77% | 58% |

At B2, that's a drop from comfortably above the 60% pass mark to well below it. The pass rate in our sample fell from 70% to 43%. The scoring logic behind this is explained in [how Goethe writing is scored](https://goethecoach.de/en/how-goethe-writing-is-scored.html).

### The four patterns behind missed Leitpunkte

Reading the grader's notes on every missed point at B2 and C1, four patterns repeat.

#### 1. The "easy" list point gets skipped

Short points like *Nennen Sie Alternativen …* or *Nennen Sie Beispiele …* were missed in 36% of cases. Demanding points like *Erläutern Sie …* or *Argumentieren Sie …* only in 14–15%. Candidates plan for the hard points and forget the easy one.

#### 2. Only one side of a two-sided point

A point like *Erläutern Sie Argumente für und gegen …* needs both sides. A frequent note: arguments *for* were there, arguments *against* were missing. That counts as not covered.

#### 3. Naming instead of doing

*Erklären Sie, wie man mit einem Gegenargument umgehen könnte* is not covered by listing a counterargument. You have to show how to respond to it. Mentioning the topic of a Leitpunkt is not the same as answering it.

#### 4. Vague coverage

"There was a problem with the service" does not cover *Erklären Sie, was für ein Problem aufgetreten ist* when the task describes a damaged product. Graders look for the concrete content the point asks for.

### How to stop missing Leitpunkte at B2 and C1

1. **Plan in reverse.** Before writing, draft one sentence for point 4, then point 3. Then write the text from point 1. The hardest-hit points now exist before you start.
2. **Budget words per point.** For a 150-word Forumsbeitrag with four points, aim for roughly 30–35 words per point plus a short intro and conclusion. Don't spend 80 words on point 1.
3. **Underline the verb and the "und".** *Nennen*, *Erklären*, *Erläutern*, *Begründen* each ask for a different action. If the point says *für und gegen*, you owe both.
4. **Signpost each point.** Use Redemittel and Konnektoren that make each point visible: *Ein weiterer Aspekt ist …*, *Als Alternative könnte man …*, *Dagegen spricht …*. Graders find what's clearly marked.
5. **Tick before you submit.** Go back to the task and tick each Leitpunkt against a specific sentence in your text. No sentence, no tick.

Practice alone doesn't fix this. Among learners who submitted three or more texts, scores rose by a median of 10 points, but the Leitpunkt miss rate stayed flat (29% → 28%). The habit only changes when you check deliberately. For the other common score killers, see [6 common Goethe writing mistakes](https://goethecoach.de/en/goethe-writing-exam-common-mistakes.html).

### Key takeaways

- About half of B2 and C1 writing texts in our data missed at least one Leitpunkt (48% and 57%), against 21% at B1.
- At B2, points 3 and 4 were missed in 43–46% of texts, points 1 and 2 in under 10%.
- The Forumsbeitrag is the riskiest task: 67% of B2/C1 forum posts missed a point.
- Texts that missed a point were not shorter. The words went to the first points.
- A missed Leitpunkt cost about 20 percentage points on average and pushed typical B2 scores below 60%.
- Short "Nennen Sie" points and two-sided "für und gegen" points are missed most often.

### Frequently asked questions

**Q: What is a Leitpunkt in the Goethe-Zertifikat?**

A: A Leitpunkt is one of the content points listed in a Schreiben task. At B2 and C1 there are usually four. Each one must be answered in your text; a point that isn't covered lowers your task-fulfilment score, no matter how good your German is.

**Q: How many Leitpunkte do I have to cover at B2?**

A: All of them. B2 and C1 tasks usually list four. In our data, texts that covered all four averaged 71% at B2, while texts that missed at least one averaged 47%, below the 60% pass mark.

**Q: Why do B2 and C1 candidates miss more Leitpunkte than B1?**

A: Tasks get longer and more argumentative. Candidates write more on the first two points and run out of attention, not words, for points 3 and 4. In our data, texts that missed a point were actually slightly longer than those that covered all of them.

**Q: Is it enough to mention a Leitpunkt briefly?**

A: No. A point counts only if you do what it asks. Naming a counterargument doesn't cover "explain how to respond to it", and giving only pros doesn't cover "arguments for and against".

**Q: How should I split my words across four Leitpunkte?**

A: For a text of around 150 words, plan roughly 30–35 words per point plus a short opening and closing. Draft a sentence for points 3 and 4 before you start, so they can't get squeezed out.

**Q: Is this data from official Goethe-Institut results?**

A: No. The figures come from 312 practice texts graded on GoetheCoach against the four official criteria. They show where learners lose points in practice, not official pass rates.

*Method: 312 graded Schreiben texts from 101 GoetheCoach learners (A1–C2, March–September 2026), of which 88 at B2 or C1 from 37 learners. Empty or off-task submissions (score 0 or under 20 words) were excluded. Each Leitpunkt was rated covered or not covered by the GoetheCoach grader. Scores are practice ratings, not official Goethe-Institut results. Some learners submitted many texts, so treat small differences with caution.*
